The '''Würzburger Trap''' is a [[chess opening]] [[Chess trap|trap]] in the [[Vienna Gambit]]. It was named around 1930 for German banker Max Würzburger.
{{AN chess|pos=toc}}

==The trap==
'''1. e4 e5 2. Nc3 Nf6 3. f4'''
:[[White and Black in chess|White]] plays the [[Vienna Gambit]].

'''3... d5'''
:Thought to be the best reply.

'''4. fxe5 Nxe4 5. d3''' 
:White also has lines beginning 5.Qf3 ([[Wilhelm Steinitz|Steinitz]]) and 5.Nf3, but neither achieves an advantage.<ref>Horowitz (1964), pp. 221–22</ref>

'''5... Qh4+{{chesspunc|?}}'''
:Initiating the trap. Black has other choices 5...Bb4 and 5...Nxc3.<ref name="Korn">Korn (1982), p. 105</ref>

'''6. g3 Nxg3 7. Nf3 Qh5 8. Nxd5 Bg4'''
:8...Nxh1? 9.Nxc7+ Kd8 10.Nxa8 leads to advantage for White.<ref name="Korn" />

'''9. Nf4'''
:White can obtain the better game with 9.Bg2 Nxh1 (9...Bxf3 10.Qxf3 Qxe5+ 11.Kd1 Nxh1 12.Bf4 Qxb2 13.Qe4+ {{chesspunc|+−}} Hamann–Schvenkrantz, Germany 1965; 10...Qxf3 11.Bxf3 Nxa1 12.Nxc7+ Kd7 13.Nxa8 Bc5 14.Bxh1 Nc6 15.Bf4{{chesspunc|±}} Arhangel'skij–Popov, USSR 1958; [[Bent Larsen|Larsen]])<ref name="ECO 2">{{cite book

'''9... Bxf3 10. Nxh5 Bxd1 11. hxg3 Bxc2?'''
:Black tries to win a [[pawn (chess)|pawn]], but instead loses a {{chessgloss|piece}}.

'''12. b3''' 
:The black [[bishop (chess)|bishop]] on c2 is trapped; White will win it by playing Kd2 or Rh2 next turn.
